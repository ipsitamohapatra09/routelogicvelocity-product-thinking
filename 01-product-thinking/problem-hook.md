## 1. Chosen scenario

**Path:** _RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)_

RouteLogic Velocity is the right scenario because it sits at the point where the product is most critical to daily operations: routing complexity is rising, service recovery matters, and the customer experience is being shaped by how well teams can handle exceptions in real time.

## 2. The strategic crisis

If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will lose ground to competitors who are shipping faster, simpler operational tools and making it easier for customers to manage route exceptions without friction. High-value accounts will start to question whether RouteLogic Velocity is still the best operational partner when their day-to-day work is slowed by an admin-first system.

> If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will lose strategic market position as customers move to tools that are easier to operate, faster to recover from disruption, and more aligned with the real work of dispatch and routing teams.

## 3. The moment of misery

The user is currently forced to manage route failures and operational exceptions outside the product: rebuilding schedules in spreadsheets, chasing updates through sticky notes and group chats, and scrambling to patch broken loads by hand. The product is not supporting the actual moment of disruption when a route starts to fall apart; it creates a workflow where the operator has to leave the system to do the work that should be happening inside it.

> The user is currently forced to leave the product and rely on spreadsheets, sticky notes, and group messages just to recover from route disruptions, fix broken loads, and coordinate the real work of the shift.

## 4. Problem hook

We must solve RouteLogic Velocity’s market risk by addressing the daily coordination failure that forces operators to work around the system instead of using it.

## 5. Value proposition

For dispatch coordinators and route operations teams, we will turn route complexity into a clear, real-time operating workflow because today’s process breaks down at the exact moment work gets messy, and customers need a fast, reliable way to recover before delays cascade across the route.

## 7. Finalized hypothesis _(complete in Module 3)_

> Based on qualitative operator pain and the recurring operational burden of manual workarounds, I believe that solving the route-exception workflow for dispatch coordinators will result in faster recovery times, lower operational churn, and better customer retention, as measured by a meaningful reduction in time-to-recover and a lower rate of missed service commitments. I will protect operational accuracy and team workload while validating the product-market fit for this initiative.
